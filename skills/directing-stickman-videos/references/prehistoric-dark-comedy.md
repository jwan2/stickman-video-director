# Style 3 — Prehistoric Hand-Drawn / Dark Comedy

## Fixed style, variable cast

Preserve the drawing grammar, not a single default face. Use crude hand-drawn 2D black outlines, white filled round heads, thin black stick limbs, expressive simple cartoon eyes/brows/mouths, animal-hide clothing, earthy ochre/brown scenery and small warm fire accents. Subtle paper texture and irregular outlines belong to this style; no photorealistic skin, glossy 3D characters or modern studio UI. Do not apply Style 1's hollow-head/no-face/no-clothes contract here.

Cast to fit the topic. Adults, children, elders and different genders can share this visual grammar without sharing a face. In Phase A provide a compact cast card for each named role: role and age range, head/body proportions, hair, eyebrows/eyes, optional facial marks, costume silhouette and one recognizable accessory. Give recurring characters at least two visible non-color differences. Do not identify gender through eyelashes alone or make every elder or child a stereotype. Keep the cast as small as the story needs; explicitly state which characters appear in each clip and forbid unintended duplicates.

New stories may use entirely new casts. Within one story, freeze each character's card and repeat its FULL description in every standalone prompt where it appears. Never use only “the same caveman” or an ellipsis as an identity lock. Lock white head fill, head/body proportions, hair, facial marks, costume and accessory. Expressions and poses may change; identity must not.

## Translate topics into concrete prehistoric situations

Identify the source's actual point, conflict and stakes first. Turn abstract mechanisms into physical tasks, incentives and props: endless recommendations may become a chief repeatedly offering snacks; workload may become a growing spear bundle; social pressure may become a small watching tribe. These are optional examples, not a recurring mandatory cast or plot. Settings can include caves, camps, forests, river crossings and rocky plains—not only the same cave bed.

Satire is fictional analogy. Do not present invented scenes as the historical origin of employment, technology or social behavior. Preserve scientific/technical explanations when relevant; if an analogy would distort the mechanism, explain it in VO or choose a clearer metaphor. Respect user requests for serious, gentle or non-comedic treatment rather than forcing ridicule.

## Story and timing

Choose the number and length of editorial shots for the story and actual narration. A premise/conflict, tempting solution/escalation and reversal/callback can guide a comic short without requiring one fixed shot per stage. The default Flow Omni 1.2 Flash generation creates ten-second source footage, while the final film keeps the requested duration and may use shorter portions or more than one source per narrative beat. Roughly 18–25 spoken English words per ten seconds can help estimate narration, not dictate every shot; leave room for reactions. A deadpan glance or held expression can be the payoff. Prefer clear action, expressive reactions, a motivated camera cut and synchronized SFX over irrelevant spectacle.

Use explicit timed physical beats and track prop ownership. State where an object starts, who hands it over and where it ends. Avoid spawning objects, teleporting stones, duplicated tools or characters changing costumes between shots. Draw a simple verbal layout, keep screen direction stable and reserve subtitle-safe space without requesting generated subtitles. In 16:9 use lateral staging; in 9:16 re-block for depth and vertical readability.

## Continuity and generation

Phase A includes cast cards, location layout, exact narration with translation when useful, music/SFX and named ending/opening states. Phase B repeats the relevant visual and cast anchors in each standalone source prompt. An intentional time jump may replace a literal match cut when named in the approved proposal and stitching guide. Choose understated primitive percussion, dry pauses or another story-appropriate score; do not inherit mandatory optimistic piano from other modes. Apply the shared `voice-continuity.md` contract: for source-generated narration, repeat the complete approved voice block in each applicable prompt; for an approved independent voice track, preserve the exact provider, voice, recording and timings in the edit and leave narrator instructions out of visual-only prompts. Deadpan character reactions and cave acoustics must not recast the narrator or add voice reverb. Narration is audio-only; no lip-sync unless approved.

When the interface supports it, reuse approved character images and prior ending frames as references. For source-generated narration, check full voice-profile repetition and contradictory audio directions when the narrator drifts; for independent narration, retain the accepted track rather than resynthesizing it automatically. Prompt repetition is not a guarantee of matching faces, voice or music, and Style 3 audio has not been separately provider-tested with the new voice block. Inspect the actual joins and preserve usable SFX. Do not promise automatic Flow operation: generation capabilities and costs require live verification and appropriate authorization. If authorized, begin with a small test and report material identity/action failures instead of spending indefinitely.

## Prompt assembly

Each prompt includes: ten-second source target and ratio → full drawing-style anchor → full cast cards for visible roles → location/layout and opening state → story-driven action or reaction and prop states → applicable audio/SFX instructions → ending state → negatives. For source-generated narration, include its exact assigned words and frozen voice block. For independent narration, keep exact words and timings in the edit plan, not visual-only prompts. Name colors naturally; never use technical color notation. Forbid visible text, subtitles, logos, speech bubbles, color swatches, extra humans, duplicate props, extra limbs and unintended identity changes. Do not forbid the cartoon faces, hair or texture that define this mode.

## Review checklist

- Source meaning retained; fiction is not mislabeled as history.
- Cast fits this story and is distinguishable without color alone.
- Every recurring role has the same complete anchor across clips.
- Correct head fill, clothing, drawing style and earthy palette.
- Actions readable; prop ownership and scene layout remain coherent.
- Payoff has breathing room; no forced comment question.
- Adjacent shots match or have an explicitly planned time jump.
- Generated footage contains no writing; subtitles are a post-production step.
- Voice/reference limitations are disclosed, not advertised as guarantees.
- Source-generated narration uses the identical full voice profile across applicable clips; an independent track retains the approved voice and original recording. Either stays separate from the prehistoric score and cast design.
