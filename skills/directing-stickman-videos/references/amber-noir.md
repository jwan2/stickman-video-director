## Shared story and visual preparation

1. Reuse the supplied topic/link/script, ratio, duration and narration language. If essential creative choices are missing, ask together; otherwise plan immediately. Thirty- and sixty-second stories use the same narrative planning process. Preserve the requested total runtime; never round it to an Omni ten-second multiple.
2. Read [directing craft](directing-craft.md) and apply its story-specific planning and review gates. Produce the director's proposal: premise, hook, causal progression and payoff; exact narration; story-specific cast and visual reference plan; shot timeline; intended audio/captions; chosen or proposed execution route. Make the first picture and opening action earn attention; each later shot must advance a question, reveal, consequence or reaction, rather than merely illustrate the same narration. Vary framing, perspective and visual emphasis for a reason. A reaction or deliberate pause is valid acting. Honor the user's requested ending and emotional payoff, including an intentional cliffhanger; do not force a discussion ending or pack decorative devices into every shot.
3. Obtain approval of this proposal before generation. Reuse approved narration and design; changes to those or the global brief require scoped reapproval, not automatic regeneration of unrelated accepted shots.
4. Before claiming that references are missing, inspect the bundled [A6 reference manifest](../assets/a6/reference-manifest.json), open its actual images and read [reference use and complete prompts](a6-reference-prompts.md). Show the suitable available example and explain its role: the accepted-film style example is not automatically the new story's approved cast or opening. Use the bundled images for drawing/light grammar, then obtain approval of the story-specific cast and shot composition. Supply fully expanded shot-specific first-frame prompts; never deliver unresolved style/cast/world lock placeholders. If files really are absent or unreadable, identify the exact missing file and recover the reference pack or arrange an approved reference-image step; never fabricate a link or substitute a multi-panel sheet as the shot opening. Do not require a new style-reference generation when a suitable image already exists.
5. Plan narrative acts separately from generated clips. For new Flow projects use **Omni 1.2 Flash, 10-second source generations** by default; this is the production preference, not a claim that 10 seconds is the model's maximum. At least `ceil(target seconds / 10)` source clips are needed to cover a film without reused material or overlaps; shorter editorial selections, handles and failed takes may require more. A 30-second film needs at least three sources, a 60-second film at least six, before those allowances. Allocate editorial time by story, legibility and actual speech: a 10-second source is not a mandate to hold one shot on screen for 10 seconds, and do not speed narration or round the finished runtime to fit. Verify the live model and duration before production; if the requested setting is unavailable, report it instead of silently switching model or duration.

Drawing grammar: compact matte-black round heads, connected short black necks, small cream-white eye marks and a compact line mouth; simplified black hands and limbs with a readable silhouette. Use angular illustrated contours, restrained cel shading, amber illumination and deep plum shadows in layered full-color settings. Keep dimensional lighting without turning the cast into glossy 3D toys or realistic people. The red-orange hood-down hoodie belongs to the current sample character, not every A6 story. Freeze the approved cast's proportions, wardrobe, palette and props within each film; vary locations according to the story rather than reusing one background.

For each shot record: editorial time and separately the 10-second source request, location and motivated camera/framing, visual purpose and change, visible cast, incoming prop/pose state, one clear main action and reaction, outgoing state, first-frame reference, separate VO interval, SFX cues and planned cut. Design the first and last visible state so adjacent shots connect without implying a different prop, action or direction; specify what viewers should understand and why this shot follows the previous one. Keep hand/prop contact and screen direction legible. For high-risk motion such as a claw gripping, lifting or releasing a toy, state the object count, identity, spatial relationships, contact and final state. Divide intricate contact into distinct shots and consider a reaction or alternate angle as an intentional story beat. Test a representative difficult shot first within the authorized budget; inspect the actual motion before producing dependent shots. Try clean cuts and alternative usable footage before retrying. Do not conceal defects by masking or replacing the actor's mouth or by pretending a frozen background is model-generated motion.

Run `python3 scripts/a6_reference_check.py` from the installed Skill directory to check the bundled resource integrity. Before image handoff/submission, record and check the reference plan as described in [the A6 prompt contract](a6-reference-prompts.md). This is a resource/structure gate, not a visual-quality verdict. Read the images and inspect actual generated results too. If the current Agent cannot read images, attach reference inputs or generate images, state the specific unavailable capability and give the complete prompt plus the actual reference files for a user-assisted handoff; do not infer that the package has no references or call that handoff a finished film. Selecting A6 or passing this check never authorizes a paid call.

## Route 1 · Flow browser (recommended)

- Verify browser-control availability and access to the intended Flow project. For a new project select **Omni 1.2 Flash**, 10 seconds per source, and verify the supported frame or reference input, mode, ratio, resolution, duration, output count and current credit quote in the live interface before each submission. Default to one output per request unless the user chooses otherwise. Compute the estimate from the actual UI price and planned generations, including any separately authorized contingency; the historical Veo price and test do not prove Omni's price or quality. Keep existing accepted clips and the user's established model choice for an ongoing project unless they request a change. Do not assume a first-and-last-frame mode is supported by 1.2 merely because another model supports it.
- Let the user complete sign-in, verification or account/billing decisions. Do not request credentials, switch accounts, recharge or upgrade automatically. Existing credits may cover generation; subscription/quota conditions and separate TTS costs still apply.
- Attach the approved shot-specific first frame using the supported input. Use a short motion prompt describing the visible action and camera; keep narration text and narrator instructions out. Request a silent non-speaking actor, with only quiet ambience if supported. Verify the entered prompt/settings before one explicit submission; retain the job/result ID and check its state before retrying after an interruption.
- Review raw results for story purpose, legibility, action, mouth, anatomy, prop count, physical contact and continuity, including motion at full speed where playback is available. For machine/animal/toy interactions look for penetrating parts, morphing, duplication, disappearance, unintended repeated movement and objects popping back into an earlier state. Do not infer success from a prompt or still contact sheet. Keep the actor performing rather than presenting or reciting the story: the mouth is a small stable line or slightly open mark without rhythmic speech motion. Offscreen-narrator wording alone did not reliably prevent speaking in earlier tests; separate voiceover is the default, not a proven mouth-stability fix. Do not repaint, mask, replace the mouth or hide the face as a workaround. Log scoped defects and the actual repair or editorial choice.
- Export valid clips and assemble locally with the shared finishing rules below. If browser control or exporting is unavailable, report the specific limitation and offer user-assisted operation. Do not silently switch providers or call a prompt package a finished film.
