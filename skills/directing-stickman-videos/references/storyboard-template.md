# Director's Proposal Contract · A1–A5

Use this contract and [story-led directing](directing-craft.md) for Phase A. Show a readable proposal and obtain approval before writing standalone model prompts. The user may already have approved a current proposal; reuse that approval within its scope. Refer to [style catalog](style-catalog.md) and the selected style's reference.

Flow **Omni 1.2 Flash**, ten-second source generations, is the default execution target; the live interface determines the available model label, mode, ratio, duration, price and resolution. A ten-second generated source is not a ten-second editorial shot or a requirement to round the film duration. Honor the user's requested total runtime and natural speech. Plan the number of source generations from the actual editorial coverage, chosen trims, handles and the complexity of actions, not a fixed equation; one source may supply shorter editorial shots and a scene may need several sources. Report estimates rather than implying that proposed clips have already been produced or their number is guaranteed.

## Resolve the story and style

Use the supplied source, audience, purpose, aspect ratio, duration, visual style and language. Reuse approved scripts and accepted narration unchanged unless a revision is authorized; for new or revised text, preserve meaning, names and supported facts. Distinguish documented claims, inference and fictional examples. Never invent statistics, quotations, product claims or a guaranteed result for a hook. If a user-authored claim needs factual review or a substantive change, identify it and obtain the user's choice; do not silently rewrite accepted voiceover.

For new English narration, roughly 20–25 words per ten seconds can be an initial estimate, not a per-shot quota. Adjust to natural speech, actual pauses and the chosen duration; simplify text before considering faster delivery. Use the user's language for planning explanations and provide a reference translation when helpful. Explicit language and voice choices prevail.

Choose a story-appropriate narrative shape. Establish an immediately readable situation or question, develop the relevant tension or discovery, and provide the payoff or ending the user requested. An explanation, an ironic visual punchline, an unresolved cliffhanger, a reaction or a quiet ending may be right; do not impose a fixed five-stage arc or mandatory discussion question. The opening should convey a meaningful visual event or change within the first few seconds, without substituting a narrated question for visible storytelling.

**Style 3:** read [prehistoric dark comedy](prehistoric-dark-comedy.md); include distinguishable cast cards, settings and prop states, with the story's setup, escalation and ironic payoff. Treat fictional analogy as such.

**Style 4:** read [Cozy Storytelling](cozy-storytelling.md); plan recognizable story-appropriate settings and purposeful wide, close or moving views. Vary the setting when the story calls for it; respect a single location or deliberate callback. Keep the cast, important props and narrator continuous. This is full-color 2D cartoon, not hand-drawn sketchbook style.

**Style 5:** read [historical cartoon](historical-cartoon.md); include its era pack, cast cards, settings, prop states and voice profile. Keep the historical drawing grammar and separate sourced facts from invented comic scenes.

## Proposal header

Present a concise working title, central premise, audience and visual hook; aspect ratio, requested duration and style; exact narration and estimated speech duration; frozen video-level voice profile from [voice continuity](voice-continuity.md); palette/environment, sound and narrative progression. Identify the selected or proposed execution route. State one coherent voice profile per recurring narrator, with explicitly approved roles for any additional speakers. Keep voice identity separate from visual emotion and music.

Use up to three saturated accent colors for Style 1 and its selected light/dark background. Apply the selected style's palette, music and composition rules. Express color names in ordinary language, without hex, RGB or other technical notation in the proposal or prompts.

## Storyboard

Write as many editorial shots as the story and narration need, rather than one row per source or a prescribed number of rows:

| Film time / narration | Narrative job and audience change | Visible action, framing and resulting state | Source/reference and planned usable interval | Audio / cut / risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|

For each shot state the subject and story-specific situation, what the viewer understands or anticipates, a legible visual action or deliberate reaction, and how the image changes the situation. Tie action and cut to the spoken idea. Plan purposeful variety in angle, scale, setting and subject; a camera push alone does not make a static shot meaningful. Allow readable pauses where they carry emotion or information. The story determines beat count and duration: no fixed three-beat timing, four-device quota or automatic two-second cut.

Record incoming/outgoing cast, object ownership, pose, contact and screen direction where relevant. Choose one primary action or reaction for a difficult generated shot and separate complex transfers, grabbing, carrying and releasing where that improves reliability. Identify failure-prone action and a simpler story-appropriate insert or cut; test a representative difficult shot before committing the rest of the authorized budget. Keep source clip lengths, editorial in/out points and narration intervals distinct.

Use deliberate connection by action, prop, direction, visual idea or a named location/time cut. Do not claim a match is seamless until actual footage has been reviewed. For 16:9, use lateral staging and negative space as helpful; for 9:16, recompose for vertical depth and platform-safe placement. Reserve room for planned captions without covering key action.

Default generated footage to no readable words, numbers, subtitles, speech bubbles, dialogue boxes or logos. If useful, list short overlays separately for post-production; do not insert technical or production annotations into generated imagery.

## Confirmation and review

Offer the current proposal for approval or a scoped revision before Phase B; do not ask again for choices and approvals already established in the current project. Do not include final model prompts in the proposal. A global brief change requires a revised proposal for the affected scope.

Before handoff, check:

- The requested total duration, style, aspect ratio, source and narration choices are preserved.
- The first image and early change are readable, and each editorial shot advances knowledge, stakes, emotion or spatial understanding.
- The story has a suitable payoff or requested ending; no CTA or five-stage template is forced.
- The timeline is voice-led; distinct editorial shots and ten-second source generations are not conflated.
- Cast, proportions, props, color/style anchors, sound and adjacent shot states are coherent.
- Risky contact or complex actions have a feasible plan, and visual effects clarify the story.
- The narration and claims are supported or deliberately supplied, and every narrator has an agreed stable profile.
- Prompt-only, structural and timing checks do not claim aesthetic quality, final playback review or a guaranteed audience completion rate.
